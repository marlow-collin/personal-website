Master's Thesis · LLM Fine-Tuning 2025

Specialising an Open Source LLM for Marine Engineering Applications within Hardware Constraints using LoRA Fine-Tuning

I fine-tuned Microsoft's Phi-3-mini for marine engineering and oceanography using LoRA and supervised fine-tuning, training on a single RTX 3090 Ti and targeting inference under 8 GB of VRAM - the kind of consumer hardware a researcher might have on board a vessel with limited connectivity.

OceanBench (domain benchmark) · METEOR
0.300 → 0.348 ▲ +0.048
MMLU (general knowledge)
0.660 → 0.645 - effectively unchanged, no catastrophic forgetting

The point was not the size of the gain but the combination: the model picked up domain knowledge without losing general capability. A second strand adapted Phi-3 to the xVal continuous number encoding; LoRA fine-tuning alone proved insufficient there, which matches the original authors' later finding that the approach needs full pre-training rather than adaptation.

ML in Finance · Replication & Extension 2023 / 24

Is Accounting Useful for Forecasting GDP Growth? Replicating and Extending a Machine-Learning Study

A two-person replication of Datar et al. (2020). I was responsible for implementation and data preparation: rebuilding the feature pipeline over 341 variables and validating the models with an expanding walk-forward scheme that respects the time-series structure of the data. Where the original feature count would not reconcile, we corresponded with one of the authors to trace the discrepancy.

Holdout MSE @ Q+4 - lower is better
baseline 1.969 → 1.187 ▲ Random Forest, all features
vs. reference paper
outperforms the original model at long horizons (Q+3, Q+4)

Beyond replicating the core finding - accounting data helps at longer horizons - the extended Random Forest beat both the Elastic Net replication and the original paper for Q+3 and Q+4. The neural network, kept deliberately within the original's constraints, underperformed the baseline throughout, which I report as such.

NLP · Data Science Project (Lead) 2023 / 24

Unified or Divided Through Sentiment? – A Party Analysis of Parliamentary Speeches

I led a team of three and handled the programming and data processing: a custom XML parser over 139 Bundestag protocols, yielding 17,198 speeches, scored with the SentiWS lexicon and compared across parties by their coefficient of variation. The question was whether sentiment within a party is homogeneous - a different angle on unity than roll-call votes.

17,198 speeches · 139 protocols · 7 party groups
H1 rejected governing parties were less unified, not more
H2 supported fringe parties showed the most homogeneous sentiment

The result ran against our first hypothesis and we kept it that way; it lines up with Sieberer (2020), who finds that governing reduces party unity. Building the parser and the scoring pipeline end to end was the core of the work.

Bachelor's Thesis · Computer Vision 2023 / 24

Simulation and Prediction of Saliency Maps for People with Impaired Visual Perception using Convolutional Neural Networks

I adapted DeepGazeIIE to predict where attention falls under three simulated impairments - reduced contrast, colour-vision deficiency, and macular degeneration - given only the original image and a flag for the impairment type. Trained on 10,000 images, evaluated on 4,012, scored with the SIM metric against computed ground truth.

SIM (contrast modification)
0.780 → 0.791 ▲ +0.011 quantitatively
Qualitative review: the gain is not real - the goal was not achieved

The numbers suggested a slight improvement, but inspecting the predictions showed the model does not meaningfully learn impairment-specific saliency - the metric was misleading. I treat that as the actual finding: the approach, as built, does not work even for the single modifications it was trained on, let alone combinations, and I can point to why (the model cannot process simultaneous modification signals, and the ground truth was simulated rather than collected from participants).
